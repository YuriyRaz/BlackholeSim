# Tidal Disruption

## Requirements

### Requirement: Tidal force computation on any body
The system SHALL compute tidal forces on any body near any black hole. The tidal acceleration across a body SHALL be: a_tidal = 2 × G × M_BH × R_body / d³, where d is the distance from the BH center to the body center.

#### Scenario: Tidal force increases as distance decreases
- **WHEN** any body moves closer to any black hole
- **THEN** the tidal force SHALL increase as 1/d³

#### Scenario: Tidal force stretches body along radial direction
- **WHEN** tidal forces are applied to a body's constituent particles
- **THEN** particles on the near side SHALL be pulled harder than particles on the far side

### Requirement: Body disruption detection
The system SHALL detect when a body is disrupted (tidal force exceeds self-gravity) and mark it as `disrupted = true`. After disruption, the body's particles SHALL be released as free bodies under gravitational influence. Star particle count SHALL follow the polytropic initializer algorithm below.

#### Scenario: Body approaches on eccentric orbit disrupts at periapsis
- **WHEN** a body on an eccentric orbit passes within the tidal disruption radius at periapsis
- **THEN** the body SHALL be marked as disrupted at closest approach

#### Scenario: Disrupted body releases particles
- **WHEN** a body is marked as disrupted
- **THEN** its constituent particles SHALL become independent bodies subject to gravity from all objects

### Requirement: Star particle count algorithm
Each star SHALL be composed of N particles generated by a polytropic initializer, where N = clamp(floor(star.mass / (0.1 × M_sun)), 200, 2000). Particle positions SHALL be distributed within the star's radius according to a Lane-Emden polytrope profile (γ = 5/3) using deterministic seeded RNG. Upon disruption, each particle inherits its position from the polytrope distribution and the star's center-of-mass orbital velocity, with no random velocity perturbation.

#### Scenario: Solar-mass star has 1000 particles
- **WHEN** a star has mass = 1.0 × M_sun
- **THEN** it SHALL be composed of 1000 particles (1.0 / 0.1 = 10, rounded to 1000)

#### Scenario: Low-mass star has 200 particles minimum
- **WHEN** a star has mass = 0.05 × M_sun
- **THEN** it SHALL be composed of 200 particles (minimum)

#### Scenario: High-mass star has 2000 particles maximum
- **WHEN** a star has mass = 100 × M_sun
- **THEN** it SHALL be composed of 2000 particles (maximum)

#### Scenario: Deterministic particle generation
- **WHEN** a star is initialized with the same seed
- **THEN** the particle positions and properties SHALL be identical across runs

### Requirement: Star deformation before disruption
The system SHALL compute deformation from tidal gradients acting on resolved particles. Deformation SHALL be proportional to (d_R / d)² where d is current distance and d_R is the disruption radius. The tidal force on each particle SHALL be computed individually based on its distance from the black hole.

#### Scenario: Star deforms before disruption
- **WHEN** a star is at distance d = 1.5 × d_R (approaching disruption radius)
- **THEN** the star SHALL exhibit deformation proportional to (d_R / d)²

#### Scenario: Star fully disrupted at tidal radius
- **WHEN** a star crosses the tidal disruption radius (d < d_R)
- **THEN** the star SHALL fully disrupt into a tidal stream of particles

### Requirement: Fallback rate computation
The system SHALL measure the fallback rate of stellar debris after disruption by counting particles returning toward the black hole. A particle is considered returning when it is within the fallback radius (2 × tidal disruption radius) and has negative radial velocity (moving inward). The fallback rate SHALL be the total returning mass divided by the timestep duration.

#### Scenario: Fallback rate increases as debris returns
- **WHEN** a star is disrupted and particles evolve under gravity
- **THEN** the fallback rate SHALL reflect the mass flux of returning particles, not a prescribed power-law curve

#### Scenario: No fallback before disruption
- **WHEN** no star has been disrupted
- **THEN** the fallback rate SHALL be zero

### Requirement: Tidal stream formation
After disruption, the system SHALL produce a tidal stream naturally from the particle orbital energy distribution. Particles from the near side of the star orbit faster (closer to BH), particles from the far side orbit slower, and the differential orbital velocities stretch the initial polytrope distribution into an elongated stream over time.

#### Scenario: Tidal stream wraps around black hole
- **WHEN** a body is disrupted and particles evolve under gravity
- **THEN** the particles SHALL form an elongated stream that wraps around the black hole over time

#### Scenario: Stream emerges from orbital energy distribution
- **WHEN** a body is disrupted
- **THEN** the stream geometry SHALL emerge from the spread in specific orbital energies among the released particles, not from pre-shaped geometry
